Missions in Voxie

Every day, Voxie gives each child three missions picked for their age: a mix of being kind, looking after themselves, thinking through real situations and stretching their brain. There are 468 missions for ages 5 to 13, and finishing them earns XP to spend on their pixel buddy.

Here's every one, so you can see what your child will get. We only show the questions here: the answers stay a surprise in the game.

Ages 5 to 7

Short, gentle missions: noticing feelings, being kind, and puzzles that use pictures and counting. 161 missions suit this age.

Self-care, kindness and mantras: small things to do today.

  • Animal walk: cross the room like a penguin, then a crab, then a frog.

  • Think of three good things from today. Tiny counts: a funny joke, a warm sock, a good biscuit. Say them or write them down.
  • Tidy one small space: your bag, a drawer or your desk. Just one. Future you says thanks.
  • Wave to yourself in the mirror and say: "Hello, brilliant!"
  • Write "Done is better than perfect" on a scrap of paper. Stick it somewhere you'll see it tomorrow.
  • Ask a grown-up to help you call or video call a grandparent or older relative. Tell them one thing about your week.
  • Ask a grown-up what they were like when they were your age. Then ask one more question about it.
  • Ask a grown-up: "Can I help?" Then do the job they give you.
  • Ask someone for help with something you've been stuck on. Homework, a game level, a stubborn knot.
  • Ask someone to teach you something they're good at: a card trick, a word in another language, a drawing trick.
  • Ask your brother, sister or a friend to pick a game. Play it their way. No changing the rules.

  • Say three nice things about yourself that aren't about how you look. "I'm funny" counts.

  • Spot a job that needs doing and do it before anyone asks. Laying the table, tidying shoes, feeding the pet.
  • Tell a friend one thing you like about being friends with them.
  • Tell someone one thing you did today that you're proud of. Bragging rights: officially allowed.
  • Tell someone you love that you love them. Out loud, a hug, a note: your choice.
  • Thank someone properly: say what they did and why it helped. "Thanks for helping with my reading, I was really stuck."

  • Try something you've never done before: write with your other hand, learn a card game, draw with your eyes shut.
