House of Kay: embracing the AI revolution

Hi, Kay here! Mum of two humans and two rescue dogs, Operations Manager based in the UK, and spare time vibe coder (are we still saying that?!). I'm now diving head first into creating the creatures and short stories that have been infiltrating my mind for as long as I can remember, and documenting the journey: the tech stack, the pipelines, the wins, the losses, the frustrations and everything in between.

The day to day routine

Balancing a full time day job, trying not to drag up two kids, navigating neurodiversity, renovating a house, and taking in rescue animals, all while carving out late night hours to code, learn AI, and build short videos from scratch. AI gave this regular person the key to unlock creative doors that always felt completely out of reach.

No fear, full speed

If AI is supposedly going to upend everything, I'd rather be wiped off the face of this earth trying to learn, utilise and have fun with it than sit back on the sidelines. Or hate on it! I want my kids to see that curiosity beats anxiety every single time.

Dark, moody and unapologetic

I gravitate towards the macabre, dark fantasy, and complex feminist narratives. I want to tackle difficult concepts head on, taking old folklore, fairy tales, nursery rhymes and fables and twisting them into thought provoking short videos and dark visual stories.

How I make the videos

This is where I break down how a short video goes from first idea to finished cut: every AI model, script and workaround I actually use, and what each one is good or terrible at. For the coding side of things, head to Vibe Coding in the content hub.

The video pipeline

Step 01

The idea

Most of my ideas arrive while I'm driving, usually sparked by nature or a piece of music, so I send Claude a voice note and it banks the idea in my Notion idea bank. I take something familiar (a fairy tale, a myth, a ritual, a childhood game) and make it quietly wrong. Ideas sit there as rough notes until one refuses to leave me alone.

Step 02

Characters and world

Before anything moves, I design the creatures and places. Each character gets a bible covering who they are, their backstory and exactly how they look, plus a set of approved reference images. I make these in Gemini, refine the best ones in Kling and fix the rest by hand. This is the step that keeps a character looking like themselves from shot to shot.

Step 03

Script, music and shot list

I break the story into beats and plan every shot. Often the music comes first: I lock the track and cut the story to its builds and drops. If there's a voiceover, I record a rough one now, so I know how long each shot needs to be.

Step 04

A still for every shot

Every shot gets a finished start frame, and usually an end frame too, approved before I go near video. Stills are free to redo and video isn't, so this is where most of the work happens. I check every still against the bibles and against each other so nothing drifts.

Step 05

Bringing it to life

Approved stills go into Kling as start and end frames, with character Elements for anything the AI loves to get wrong (four-fingered hands, I'm looking at you). I allow three attempts per shot. After that, I fix it in the edit instead.

Step 06

Edit, sound and grade

Everything comes together in CapCut, vertical 9:16, exported at 24fps. Glitches get hidden with failing-light flickers, black frames and hard cuts. Sound effects, music and a shared colour grade make all the shots feel like one world.

The tech stack

Planning & helpers

Notion Claude VS Code

Ideas usually hit me while I'm driving, so I send Claude voice notes and it banks them in my Notion idea bank. Claude also keeps the character bibles and shot lists up to date and checks every still for consistency. VS Code is where it all lives.

Images

Gemini (Nano Banana) Kling 3.0 Omni Python

Gemini is where everything starts, because it's free and quick to iterate on. Kling sharpens the best ones. Anything the AI keeps getting wrong, like goat pupils, monograms or the wrong number of fingers, I fix by hand with Python.

Video

Kling 3.0 Kling Elements ffmpeg

Kling animates the approved stills from start and end frames. Elements keep hands and faces consistent between shots, and ffmpeg trims, retimes and fixes judder.

Sound & edit

CapCut Pixabay Shure MV6 Python

CapCut for the edit and grade, Pixabay for music, and a Shure MV6 for voiceovers. I also have Python scripts that make flickers, synced electrical buzz and a broken music box.

The characters

Aske

Piskie · Happy Face

Pronounced "ASK-uh", the Norwegian and Danish word for ash. When she was a child, a fire took her home and her family and broke her wings as she escaped. After years of grief she decided to take happiness back. Now she walks into the Office of Untimely Endings with an antique shotgun that fires colour powder. Her revenge is joy.

Meet Aske · 4 images

Gristle

Bogle · Happy Face

About 175 years old. He was left on a butcher's doorstep as a baby and paid for his keep with two fingers of his left hand. He has never known love and scrubs his skin until it's red raw. Yet he turns up to work every day immaculate in his dark green suit, and stamps horrific early deaths on babies' certificates without even looking.

Meet Gristle · 5 images

Art

Young Bogle · Happy Face

Only 35, which is young for a bogle, and wearing the hand-me-down suit of the uncle who got him the job. Art doesn't belong at the Office of Untimely Endings. He feels pangs of guilt he can't name, and hovers over the stamp rack looking for the gentlest ending he can find.

Meet Art · 4 images

The Bogle clerks

Office Bogles · Happy Face

The bored, joyless bureaucrats who fill the towering counters of the counting hall. They aren't cruel like Gristle or troubled like Art; they stopped caring a long time ago. Clerk 1, the dark slate bogle, is the first one Aske shoots.

Meet the clerks · 6 images

Morrowveils

Watcher Species · Happy Face

Nosey watcher creatures, part moth and part small furry mammal, with glossy black button eyes, tiny branching antlers and a symbol grown into their skulls. They gather in the fog wherever drama is about to happen, because they feed on it. Over time they change, depending on whether they feast on joy or on tragedy.
